## 3. Event ID Reference

**4625 — Account failed to log on**
Records every failed authentication attempt. Key fields: account name, failure reason, source machine, and logon type. Logon Type 2 = interactive (console). Logon Type 7 = workstation unlock.

**4740 — User account locked out**
Generated by Active Directory when the failed logon count for an account reaches the configured threshold. Identifies the account and the machine from which the locking attempt originated.

**4722 — User account enabled**
Generated when a previously disabled or locked account is re-enabled. Records the account affected and the admin who performed the action. Note: standard account unlock typically produces Event ID 4767. The presence of 4722 here suggests the helpdesk admin used the "enable account" action rather than the dedicated unlock function — functionally equivalent in outcome but worth noting for audit precision. Verify in AD audit trail whether 4767 was also generated.

**4624 — Account successfully logged on**
Confirms successful authentication. Presence of this event immediately after the admin action confirms the user's password was valid throughout — the lockout was caused by failed attempts, not a forgotten or expired password.

## 4. Sequence of Events — Narrative

jsmith arrived at DESKTOP-FB001 at approximately 08:02 and attempted to log in interactively. The password entered was incorrect, generating the first authentication failure (4625). A second attempt at 08:04 also failed. Active Directory's Account Lockout Policy triggered at 08:06, locking the account after the threshold was breached. At 08:07, jsmith attempted to unlock the workstation (Logon Type 7), unaware the account was now locked — this failed immediately with the updated reason "Account locked out." jsmith contacted the helpdesk. At 08:22, helpdesk-admin enabled the account in Active Directory. jsmith successfully authenticated at 08:23, 21 minutes after the initial failure.

---

## 5. Root Cause

**Primary cause:** jsmith entered an incorrect password on two successive interactive logon attempts, meeting the configured Account Lockout Threshold and triggering an automatic lockout.

**Evidence:**
- Both 4625 events prior to lockout carry reason "Unknown username or bad password" — not "Account locked out", confirming the password was wrong at time of entry.
- All events originate from a single source machine (DESKTOP-FB001) with Logon Type 2, ruling out remote or automated attack.
- Successful logon (4624) immediately after admin intervention confirms the password was known to the user and valid — mistyping, not forgetting, was the cause.

**Contributing factor:** The Account Lockout Threshold appears to be set to 2 failed attempts. This is significantly lower than Microsoft's baseline recommendation of 10 attempts and is likely increasing the frequency of lockout incidents across the organisation.

---

## 6. Five Why Analysis

**Problem statement:** jsmith was locked out of DESKTOP-FB001 and was unable to work for approximately 21 minutes.

---

**Why 1 — Why was the account locked out?**
Because Active Directory's Account Lockout Policy automatically locked the account after the configured threshold of failed logon attempts was reached.

---

**Why 2 — Why did the failed logon attempts reach the threshold?**
Because jsmith entered an incorrect password on two consecutive interactive logon attempts at DESKTOP-FB001, triggering the lockout at the second failure.

---

**Why 3 — Why did jsmith enter the incorrect password twice?**
Most likely because jsmith had recently had their password changed (or changed it themselves) and had not yet committed the new credential to memory, or mistyped under time pressure. There is no evidence of a forgotten password — the correct password was used successfully at 08:23 without a reset.

> *[Action: Confirm with jsmith whether a password change occurred in the 24–48 hours prior to the incident. Check AD password last-set attribute for the account.]*

---

**Why 4 — Why did two incorrect attempts result in a lockout?**
Because the domain Account Lockout Threshold is configured at 2 failed attempts. This is an unusually restrictive setting. Microsoft's Security Baseline recommends a minimum of 10 attempts to balance security with usability and reduce unnecessary lockout volume.

> *[Action: Review the current Account Lockout Policy via Group Policy Management Console. Compare against Microsoft Security Baseline and DWP policy standards.]*

---

**Why 5 — Why is the lockout threshold set so low?**
The rationale for the threshold of 2 is not documented in any accessible policy record. It may have been set historically to satisfy a specific audit requirement, adopted from an overly conservative baseline, or misconfigured and never reviewed. The absence of a documented policy decision means there is no clear owner or review cadence for this setting.

> *[Action: Identify the GPO owner. Schedule a formal review of the Account Lockout Policy with the security team. Document the decision and link it to the relevant DWP security policy.]*

## 10. Preventive Measures

1. **Policy review:** Align Account Lockout Threshold with Microsoft's recommended baseline (10 attempts) unless a documented security justification requires a lower value.
2. **Observation window:** Set Account Lockout Observation Window to at least 15 minutes to ensure counters reset for genuine mistyping rather than attacks.
3. **Self-service password reset:** If not already deployed, evaluate Azure AD / Entra SSPR or an on-premises equivalent so users can unlock their own accounts without helpdesk involvement, reducing resolution time and helpdesk load.
4. **Awareness:** Brief users on the lockout policy — knowing the threshold exists encourages more careful logon behaviour, especially after a password change.
5. **Alerting:** Configure SIEM or MDM alerting on repeated 4740 events for the same account or source machine within a short window to detect credential-stuffing or brute-force attacks that would look similar in the log.

---

## 11. Lessons Learned

- A lockout threshold of 2 is operationally disruptive and disproportionate to the security risk of interactive console logons, where physical access is already a prerequisite.
- The 16-minute gap between lockout and helpdesk resolution (08:06 to 08:22) represents avoidable productivity loss that self-service tooling would eliminate.
- Event ID 4722 appearing in place of 4767 indicates a procedural inconsistency in the helpdesk unlock workflow that may complicate automated audit correlation.
